# Purpose & Review Justification

## The gap this paper closes
Today's AI strategy content splits in two:
- **Too technical** — lab blogs, papers, model cards. Useful, but readers without an ML background can't translate them into capital allocation.
- **Too consumer-facing** — op-eds, hype takes, vendor marketing. Easy to read, useless for decisions.

Nothing is written *for capital-rich, conceptually-light operators*. Many of them already control the exact assets that will matter most in the next phase of AI — proprietary data, distribution, regulated-vertical incumbency, energy, real estate — and yet are watching from the sidelines because no one has handed them a usable map.

This paper exists to give them that map.

## Why secondary research
- Primary sources already exist and are public: lab releases, earnings calls, capex disclosures, analyst reports, founder essays, regulatory texts.
- Original empirical work would take 6+ months. Synthesis can ship in 4–6 weeks.
- The contribution is **the frame**, not new data. The frame is what is missing in the market.

## Honesty about stance
The paper is opinionated by construction. The working hypotheses — [[H1_L0-L7-ladder]], [[H2_u-curve-of-value]], [[H3_orthogonal-axes-under-priced]] — are starting scaffolding. The research may reshape them, and if it does, the paper says so. Pretending to neutrality would be dishonest given the kickoff intent.

## What would make this paper fail
- If it reads as a defense of a pre-baked thesis rather than an inquiry.
- If it skips the bear case and skeptic voices.
- If readers come away with vibes instead of moves they can execute.
