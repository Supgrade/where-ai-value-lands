# Business ideas

> [!abstract] One-line
> Speculative business / company ideas the author is considering building or testing. Each page describes one concrete business: what it sells, to whom, why it would work, what conditions it depends on, what would kill it.

## How this folder differs from neighbours

- **`06_ideas/`** — *observed* use-case patterns. How AI is actually being used on the ground today, anchored in ingested sources (Deloitte ERI, OECD SME). Descriptive.
- **`02_hypothesis/`** — *research hypotheses* about where value lands across the agentic AI stack. Project-paper-level claims (H1–H6) with evidence accumulation rules. Analytical.
- **`07_business-ideas/`** (this folder) — *speculative businesses* the author might build. Operator-facing. Prescriptive at the level of "could I do this thing." Each page is a candidate company, not a research claim.

A business-idea page is the *application* of one or more hypotheses to a concrete operator move. It draws on the wiki for its premises and is allowed to be wrong — its job is to be sharp enough that the author can decide whether to invest his own time in it.

## Page contract

Each page in this folder includes:

- **Frontmatter** — `status` (`speculative`, `under-investigation`, `pursuing`, `abandoned`), `tags`, `last-updated`.
- **One-line pitch** — what is sold, to whom, in one sentence.
- **The mechanism** — why the buyer pays; the wedge.
- **Required conditions** — what must be true for this to work; the load-bearing assumptions.
- **GTM options** — the candidate go-to-market motions, not just one.
- **What would kill it** — the bear case in concrete terms, drawn from wiki concepts.
- **Open questions** — what the author does not yet know.
- **Related** — wikilinks to the hypotheses and concepts this business sits on top of.
- **Referenced by** — reverse-link index (maintained by `/lint`).
