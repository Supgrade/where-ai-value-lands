# SME Optimiser — Cross-Functional Off-the-Shelf AI Stack

The mid-level SME adoption pattern: multiple off-the-shelf AI tools deployed across multiple departments with growing co-ordination. The firm has not yet built or customized any AI itself, but it has internalized the practice of *picking the right off-the-shelf tool for the task* — and routing data and outputs across them.

## Canonical examples from [[oecd-sme-ai-adoption-2025]]

- **Artisan bakery, Sèvres, France.** Already used Shopify, Trustpilot, Google My Business, Google/Meta Ads, 3D printing, and graphic design software. AI layer added on top: ChatGPT for customer-service responses, blog and social media content, recipe optimization, HR-process improvement. Midjourney for visual creation, graphic retouching, product ideation. Founder quote: "*the integration process was completely self-taught, driven by our curiosity and our desire to innovate.*"
- **Bag manufacturer, Brescia, Italy.** Flexible-packaging SME using ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, and machine-learning models to streamline production analytics, automate operational flows, solve novel tasks. Government support received. Main reported challenge: *integration of AI within the broader company ecosystem*.

## The pattern in abstract

1. **High prior digital maturity.** Optimisers run the operational stack on SaaS — e-commerce, CRM, ads, analytics. AI tools plug in alongside.
2. **Tool diversity, not depth.** ChatGPT for text, Gemini or Claude for alternative reasoning, Midjourney or Stable Diffusion for visuals, Copilot for code/spreadsheet automation. The firm picks the right tool for each task.
3. **Cross-functional scope.** Marketing, customer service, HR, operations, R&D — AI is no longer confined to one team. *Growing* co-ordination — shared prompts, prompt libraries, sometimes governance — but not yet enterprise-wide.
4. **Workflow co-ordination is the active project.** The bag manufacturer's stated challenge — "integrating AI within the broader company ecosystem" — is the defining bottleneck for this pattern. Tools work individually; making them work together is the next problem.
5. **Still no custom models.** No fine-tuning, no proprietary data integration into model training. The firm is renting capability across many functions.

## What makes this category load-bearing

- **It is the modal "successful" SME adopter.** Optimisers represent the next-step destination from Novice — the achievable bull-managerial-case outcome for most SMEs over the next 2–3 years.
- **It is the wide-but-shallow trap.** Most Optimisers will likely stall here. Moving from off-the-shelf cross-functional to customized or enterprise-wide requires investment thresholds (compute, data engineering, integration consultants) that few SMEs can clear without external support. This is the SME analogue of the [[scaling-gap]].
- **It is where consultancies sell the most.** Per [[taker-shaper-maker]], the consultancy commercial interest is precisely to move firms from Taker (Optimiser) to Shaper (Explorer/Champion). The Optimiser-to-Explorer transition is the consultancy upsell.

## Implications for the U-curve

The Optimiser shows the **maximum value-capture an SME can reach without owning anything**. They orchestrate other people's models across their own workflow. The application-side surplus they capture is real — measurable productivity in marketing, customer service, internal ops — but the *recurring rent* they pay (subscriptions to ChatGPT Pro, Midjourney, Copilot, Claude, Shopify-AI, etc.) is non-trivial. Roughly: more rent, more capability, more surplus *transferred upward* to model and platform vendors.

If most SMEs reach Optimiser and stay there, the steady-state economics of the application layer in the SME dimension look like: **a stable per-firm subscription stack**, with the firm capturing a bounded productivity gain and the model/platform vendors capturing recurring revenue. This is consistent with the [[middle-layer-defensibility]] argument that distribution-embedded platforms (Shopify, Microsoft, Adobe) are well-positioned to capture much of the surplus by bundling AI into the tools SMEs already pay for.
