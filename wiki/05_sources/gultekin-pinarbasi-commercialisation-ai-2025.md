# Gültekin et al. — Commercialisation of AI: A Research on Entrepreneurial Companies (2025)

> [!note] Partial ingest
> Per author's instruction, **not folded fully into the wiki**. Ingested only for what bears on [[weber-taxonomy-2026-gaps]]. The paper's main thrust (Turkish-ecosystem-specific commercialisation challenges and BAH-vs-Claessens process models) is left unmaterialised — it is a development-economics / national-ecosystem study, not a taxonomy paper.

## Citation

Gültekin, D. G., Pinarbasi, F., Yazici, M., & Adiguzel, Z. (2025). **Commercialisation of artificial intelligence: a research on entrepreneurial companies with challenges and opportunities.** *Business Process Management Journal*, 31(2), 605–630. Emerald. DOI: 10.1108/BPMJ-10-2023-0836. Received 26 October 2023; accepted 23 August 2024.

## Why this paper exists in the wiki

It is the most recent (2025) peer-reviewed empirical study in the wiki that **operationally uses Weber et al.'s (2022) four-pattern AI-startup taxonomy as a classifier**, on a sample collected *after* the cutoff of the original Weber paper. It therefore provides a small but specific test of how the taxonomy holds up as new evidence accumulates — and is the first independent confirmation in this wiki that researchers two-and-a-half years downstream still reach for the Weber classification when they need one. See [[weber-ai-startup-business-models]] and [[ai-startup-business-archetypes-weber]] for the classifier.

## Method and sample (one paragraph)

Semi-structured online interviews with **10 founders / C-level executives** of AI-as-core-product startups in Istanbul and Antalya technoparks. Interviews conducted **November 2022** (≈one month before ChatGPT's launch — the sample is effectively pre-FM-era operationally, even though the paper was published 2025). MAXQDA20 structured qualitative content analysis. Sample composition: 5 computer vision, 4 predictive data analytics, 1 language processing. Firm sizes: median 14 staff, < 50 customers, except the single language-processor (LP1) which has > 100,000 customers via consumer transcription/voice service.

## What it says about [[weber-taxonomy-2026-gaps]]

> [!important] Headline contribution
> On re-reading, this paper's most decisive contribution to the gaps diagnostic is **not** that it weakly evidences the existing six dimensions, but that it **surfaces a seventh dimension Weber's morphological method could not encode**: a *process* dimension — *commercialisation motion / GTM posture* — distinct from the *structural* channel-geometry of the original gap 4. Section §5 of the paper (BAH vs Claessens) is the empirical anchor; the dimension now lives on the gaps page as **gap 7**. This is the first gap-page dimension empirically derived from an ingested source rather than from wiki-internal triangulation.

### Where it confirms / qualifies the diagnostic

**Gap 1 — Model sourcing** (in [[weber-taxonomy-2026-gaps]] §1). **Weak indirect confirmation.** Out of 10 firms, **only one** (CV1, computer vision 3D scene generation) classifies as [[ai-development-facilitator]]; **nine** classify as [[ai-charged-product-service-provider]] or [[ai-data-analytics-provider]]. This is consistent with the wiki's reading that the facilitator pattern is the most-squeezed-by-FM-commoditization slot, even on a sample still pre-ChatGPT. But the paper does **not** ask the model-sourcing question itself — the dimension remains under-evidenced and the Strand A ingest is still needed.

**Gap 4 — Distribution architecture, channel-geometry slice only** (§4). The single B2C self-serve firm (LP1) is the only firm in the sample with > 100,000 customers; all nine B2B firms have < 50. Direction consistent with [[distribution-moat]] — but on close reading this is **not actually about channel geometry**: LP1 is *both* B2C and self-serve, while the nine B2B firms are *both* B2B and demo-led / network-channelled — so the dataset cannot separate the channel effect from the motion effect. The paper does not address the channel-geometry characteristic set on the gaps page (direct / marketplace-listed / platform-embedded / wholesale-API / FM-store / OSS-distributed). **The real signal in §4 turns out to be process, not channel — see gap 7 below.**

**Gap 7 — Commercialisation motion / GTM posture** (§5, §5.1, §5.3 — *new dimension*). **Strong empirical support.** Section §5 contrasts two literature commercialisation process models:

- **BAH (Booz, Allen & Hamilton 1982)** — linear, sequential: new product strategy → idea generation → screening → development → commercialisation (last stage) → post-launch review. Marketing arrives only after development. The dev-first paradigm.
- **Claessens (2015)** — interleaved: idea generation → screening → concept dev & testing → **marketing strategy & business analysis** → product development → **market testing** → commercialisation → post-launch review. Marketing-strategy and market-test phases occur *before* full commercialisation.

The paper finds Turkish AI startups **default to BAH** despite **none of them having a deliberate approach or model**:

> *"Choosing the commercialisation process seems not to be a conscious or intentional decision on businesses' part, it is rather the consequence of the previously implemented practical solutions. Many start-ups tend to follow the BAH model, which places less emphasis on marketing activities... no matter which model was used in practice, the respondents stressed that the process lacked clear-cut steps; it was depicted as a fuzzy process."*

This is the **fuzzy / emergent** motion: nominally BAH-like in outcome (dev-heavy, marketing-light), but with no formal process at all in practice. Section §5.1 adds an orthogonal **internal-vs-external** spectrum (own resources / customer-driven iteration vs investor- and partner-led GTM). Sections §5.3 and §5.4 surface auxiliary motion-level mechanics: state-program contests as primary entry channel, demo-as-sales-tool, international-body lobbying as legitimacy-building, and the B2B sales-cycle reality ("one or two sales per year is good" — CV2).

The empirical signal is sharp: of 10 firms, only one (LP1) is on the self-serve / B2C-direct motion; it is the **only** firm with mass-market reach. The other nine — across computer vision, predictive data analytics, and one development facilitator, across founding years 2000–2021, across staff counts 2–46 — are all on the dev-led, demo-driven, contest-channelled motion, and all have < 50 customers regardless of AI capability or industry. **In a sample of 10 this is anecdotal**, but it directionally points the same way as the [[distribution-moat]] thesis (Cursor's self-serve motion is half its moat). It is also a *process* dimension that Weber's morphological method **could not have surfaced** — only a qualitative interview method can.

This was the missing dimension in the original six-gap diagnostic. Promoted to gap 7 on the gaps page.

**Gap 6 — Ethics / safety / compliance as product** (§6). **Confirms the dimension exists in the discourse but is not yet productized in this sample.** Participants explicitly raise transparency, accountability, and "social benefit integration" as concerns for sustainability (§5.4):
> *"The focus is on improving transparency and accountability in AI algorithms to prevent harm to society. This will help build trust and open up more possibilities for using AI."*
None of the ten firms sells ethics, safety, or compliance as a product feature; the concern is articulated as a *macro* worry about the field, not as a *micro* product surface. This independently corroborates Weber et al.'s own 2021 expectation that ethics should have surfaced as a dimension but didn't — the asymmetry between **concern-level salience** and **product-surface absence** is a stable feature across two pre-2023 samples on different geographies. Strand A ingest still required to evidence post-2023 ethics-as-product emergence.

### Where it has nothing to say (gaps the paper does not inform)

- **Gap 2 — Autonomy level.** The interview guide does not distinguish copilot / agent / autonomous-worker. The four-pattern classification absorbs everything into "Autonomous robots & bots" (none of the sample) or "Process & task support" (most of the sample). No 2026 evidence here.
- **Gap 3 — Orchestration depth.** Pre-ChatGPT sample; chains and multi-agent systems are not in the design space.
- **Gap 5 — Data flywheel.** The paper repeatedly emphasises **customer feedback** as the dominant resource (§5.2) but this is *human conversational feedback*, not the behavioural / trajectory data flywheel of [[continual-learning-paradigm]] or [[distribution-moat]]. No instances of the Cursor-style behavioural-data accumulation in the sample.

## Net effect on [[weber-taxonomy-2026-gaps]] confidence

- **Adds a new dimension — gap 7 (Commercialisation motion / GTM posture)** — empirically anchored in §5 of this paper. First gap-page dimension derived from an ingested source.
- **Splits the original gap 4** into a pure channel-geometry slice (under-evidenced here) and the process slice that became gap 7.
- **Weakly evidences gaps 1, 6** (under-population of facilitator slot; ethics-as-concern-not-product asymmetry confirmed across two geographies).
- **Leaves gaps 2, 3, 5 unaddressed** (sample pre-dates ChatGPT).
- **Does not retire any dimension.**
- **Does not validate the fifth-archetype claim** (agentic-workflow provider) — the sample has none, consistent with a Nov 2022 cutoff.

The page's `Status` and `Confidence` should remain *working-draft* until a 2024–2026 post-ChatGPT taxonomy study is ingested (Strand A) — but with the asterisk that **gap 7 now has a peer-reviewed empirical anchor**, which none of the other six dimensions has. The taxonomy has *not* been abandoned by the research community; it is still the off-the-shelf classifier of choice in 2024 BISE-adjacent venues — but it now has a documented process-dimension blind spot.

## Other notable observations (logged for completeness, not promoted)

These are interesting but bear on the user's *Türkiye-specific* commercialisation questions, not on the gaps diagnostic, so they live here as a record rather than as new concept pages:

- The paper distinguishes BAH (1982) and Claessens (2015) commercialisation process models and finds Turkish AI startups **default to BAH** (technical-development-heavy, marketing-light) despite none having a "deliberate approach or model for commercialization."
- State-program funding (KOSGEP, TÜBİTAK) supports R&D but not marketing/sales — a structural ecosystem mismatch.
- "Made in Türkiye" bias from both domestic and international buyers / investors; several firms establish secondary entities in Germany to bypass it.
- Customer-as-co-creator is the dominant resource in the sample — but as human iterative feedback, not as a data asset.

If a future paper section needs **emerging-market AI-startup commercialisation patterns**, this source is the anchor. It is not the anchor for taxonomy work.
