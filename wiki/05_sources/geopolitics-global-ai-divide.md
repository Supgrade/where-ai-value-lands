# The Geopolitics and Governance of the Global AI Divide

Internal synthesis covering the tripartite geopolitical architecture of the global AI ecosystem (US / China / EU), the three governance models, and the divergent optima each engineers in the AI value stack.

> [!note] Type
> This is a secondary-research synthesis assembled in `raw/`, not a single primary paper. It braids Anu Bradford ([[bradford-digital-empires]]), Jeffrey Ding ([[ding-diffusion-marathon]]), Helen Toner, Kai-Fu Lee ([[lee-01ai-pivot]]), and the LSE *Brussels Mirage* legal scholarship into one frame. Primary sources are listed in [[#Annotated source list]].

## Central claim

The AI value stack does **not** have a single global shape. The optimum is engineered exogenously by the host bloc's capital structure, regulatory philosophy, and geopolitical constraints. See [[divergent-value-stack-optima]].

- **United States** — top-heavy optimum. Value concentrates at hardware (Nvidia) + foundation-model/cloud layer (Microsoft/OpenAI, Google, AWS, Anthropic). Strategic moat: capital + compute scale. Strategy: closed-API frontier models, AGI sprint framing.
- **China** — bottom-heavy optimum. Value pushed downstream to application, B2B enterprise integration, Global-South infrastructure. Strategic moat: rapid diffusion + open-weight commoditization that destroys the model layer's economic value. Forced into this shape by US chip embargoes.
- **European Union** — regulatory-centric optimum. Value attempted at the governance + sovereign-infrastructure layer. Strategic moat (theoretical): compliance as global standard via [[brussels-effect-and-mirage]]. Currently functions more as handicap than moat.

## Four structural axes of divergence

Per Bradford ([[digital-empires-tripartite]]):

1. **Locus of value extraction** — US: closed APIs + cloud rent. China: open-weight B2B deployment. EU: sovereign infra + compliance licensing.
2. **Innovation vs. diffusion** ([[diffusion-vs-innovation]]) — US optimizes for frontier breakthroughs; China is pivoting to mass diffusion across industrial sectors.
3. **Model accessibility** — US: proprietary weights as national-security IP. China: open weights as asymmetric weapon ([[open-weight-asymmetry]]) — *"Android of the AI era"*.
4. **Global export mechanism** — US exports private corporate power; China exports infrastructure power (Digital Silk Road); EU exports regulatory power (Brussels Effect).

## Notable empirical anchors

- **DeepSeek "Sputnik moment"** — V4/R1 trained on H800 chips at a fraction of Western compute cost; explicitly optimized for Huawei Ascend. Folded into [[open-weight-asymmetry]].
- **01.AI pivot from B2C → B2B** — Kai-Fu Lee's strategic reorientation toward banking, insurance, mining, energy. See [[lee-01ai-pivot]].
- **EU AI Act backlash** — open letter from European founders and VCs (Kry, 20VC, Voi, Synthesia) demanding implementation pause. Compliance overhead drains scarce VC capital. See [[brussels-effect-and-mirage]].
- **Global AI Index (Tortoise)** — US + China dominate; Germany 8th, France 11th, Sweden 25th. Quantitative backbone for EU structural deficit.
- **Sovereign AI footprint** — Denmark (Ozempic-funded supercomputer for biotech), Italy (LLM for government workers), Sweden (rebuild). See [[sovereign-ai]].

## How this bears on the wiki's hypotheses

**[[H2_u-curve-of-value]] — major refinement.** The single-global-U is wrong. There appear to be *three different shapes*: top-heavy (US), bottom-heavy (China), regulatory-centric (EU). The U-curve, if it holds at all, may be a Western-stack artifact. See [[divergent-value-stack-optima]] for full treatment.

**[[H1_L0-L7-ladder]] — not directly challenged.** The ladder is a substrate-ownership map; the geopolitical layer sits orthogonal. But L6–L7 (own model + own compute + own energy) maps neatly onto China's domestic-substitution drive (Huawei Ascend, sovereign chips) and EU's "AI factories" — both are L7 plays under different banners.

**[[bear-case-synthesis]] — partial counterweight.** [[zitron-circular-economics]] argues the US bottom of the U is propped up by recycled VC. This source independently argues that the same US bottom is propped up by an *over-indexed* national-security framing (Toner's critique). Two different attacks, same target: the US infrastructure-layer rent extraction may be more fragile than it looks.

## Contradictions and open questions surfaced

- **Is the EU AI Act a moat or a handicap?** Source argues *handicap in short-to-medium term; potential moat in the long term if global enterprise demand matures around verifiable safety*. Existing [[H2_u-curve-of-value]] is silent on this — needs incorporation.
- **Does the "Brussels Effect" actually work for AI?** The LSE *Brussels Mirage* paper exposes that compliance form travels but rights-protective substance does not (case study: predictive policing in India). Undermines the optimistic EU thesis.
- **Is the open-weight commoditization durable, or a temporary embargo workaround?** If US export controls relaxed tomorrow, would China revert to closed-weight competition? Source assumes not (state-directed strategy), but worth pressure-testing.
- **What happens when blocs collide?** The source treats the three optima as parallel; the open question is what happens at the seams — e.g., European startups building on Chinese open weights to escape both US API rents and EU compliance drag.
