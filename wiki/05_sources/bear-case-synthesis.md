# AI Redistribution of Economic Surplus — The Skeptics and Bear Case

> [!info] Citation
> Internal synthesis document drafted in preparation for *Where Value Lands*. Surveys and braids together four priority skeptic works: [[acemoglu-simple-macroeconomics]], [[goldman-sachs-too-much-spend]], [[marcus-world-models-failure]], [[zitron-circular-economics]]. Raw file: `raw/AI Redistribution of Economic Surplus — The Skeptics and Bear Case.md`.

## Central claim
The dominant narrative — that generative AI and AGI will radically redistribute economic surplus, double GDP trajectories, and rewrite labor economics — is not supported by rigorous evidence across four interlocking pillars: cognitive/architectural limits, labor-economic ceilings on productivity, ROI shortfall versus a ~$1T capex cycle, and a structurally circular product-layer economy. The acute risks are not sentient AI or mass job destruction — they are **capital misallocation, stalled aggregate productivity, and widening capital-labor inequality**.

## The four pillars

1. **Capability overestimation.** LLMs are sophisticated next-token predictors, not reasoning agents. They lack causal world models and compositionality. The "scaling law" hypothesis underpinning trillion-dollar capex is contradicted by diminishing returns at the frontier. See [[scaling-wall]].
2. **Productivity paradox.** A task-based framework grounded in Hulten's theorem caps 10-year US TFP gains at ~0.55–0.71% — orders of magnitude below McKinsey/GS optimistic forecasts. Only ~4.6% of US tasks are profitably automatable. See [[task-based-framework]].
3. **ROI shortfall.** ~$1T in hyperscaler+semi capex is not matched by enterprise-grade value creation. AI is a high-cost solution applied to low-value tasks, inverting the historical pattern of disruptive tech. Energy and grid constraints compound the problem. See [[ai-capex-cycle]] (folded into [[circular-ai-economy]]).
4. **Circular product-layer economics.** 70–80% of hyperscaler "AI revenue" is consumed by OpenAI and Anthropic, themselves funded by VC and recycling that cash back to hyperscalers. Application startups operate at deeply inverted margins (Perplexity ~164% of revenue on compute). See [[circular-ai-economy]].

## Key quantitative anchors worth preserving
- ~19.9% of US labor tasks theoretically exposed to gen AI; only ~23% of those are profitably automatable → ~4.6% of all US tasks.
- Average task-level cost saving: ~15.4% → cumulative 10-year TFP ceiling 0.55–0.71%.
- Hyperscaler historical capex ~$800B; 2026–2027 additional ~$1.7T.
- Revenue required to break even: ~$3T; for positive ROI: $6T+.
- Combined annual revenue of MSFT+META+AMZN+GOOG (all products): ~$1.599T.
- OpenAI: ~50% of revenue on inference + ~75% on training; burned $9B to lose $5B in one year.
- Perplexity: ~164% of revenue spent on compute in 2024.
- 70–80% of MSFT/AMZN "AI compute capacity" consumed by OpenAI + Anthropic.

## Stance — what this contributes to the inquiry
- Provides the **bear-case anchor** the paper plan flagged in [[05_open-questions]].
- Validates a specific shape of [[H2_u-curve-of-value]]: it strongly supports the **middle dies** claim (mid-stack startups bleed margin) but **threatens the bottom**: hyperscalers may be holding rapidly depreciating, mispriced assets. The bottom of the U may not be safe either.
- Pressures [[H1_L0-L7-ladder]]: if the scaling wall is real, the autonomy gains imagined at L4/L5 may never materialize at production reliability, and L7 (intelligence as commodity) becomes a bet on a paradigm shift, not a glide path.

## Contradictions and tensions surfaced
- The U-curve hypothesis assumes the bottom of the stack is durably profitable. Zitron's circular-economy thesis argues the bottom is currently propped up by VC pass-through, not enterprise revenue. If true, **the U is upside-down: middle dies, but bottom dies too once the music stops**.
- Acemoglu's productivity ceiling implies a smaller pie to fight over. If TFP gains over 10 years are ~0.7%, the "redistribution of economic surplus" framing of the paper needs to grapple with the possibility that there is **no surplus to redistribute** — only a reshuffling of capital from VCs to GPU vendors.
- The "killer app has not emerged" critique directly threatens any application-layer winner-take-most thesis embedded in [[H3_orthogonal-axes-under-priced]].
