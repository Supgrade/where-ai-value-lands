## Core argument (as inherited)
The AI application and product layer is a **financial ouroboros**:
1. Venture capital funds application startups (Cursor, Perplexity, etc).
2. Startups immediately pay foundation models (OpenAI, Anthropic) for API access — many at deeply inverted margins (Perplexity ~164% of revenue on compute in 2024).
3. Foundation models pay hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP) for compute — OpenAI ~50% revenue on inference + ~75% on training.
4. Hyperscalers report this VC cash as "AI revenue" to Wall Street, justifying further capex.
5. ~70–80% of hyperscaler AI capacity is consumed by just two entities: OpenAI and Anthropic.

Genuine non-tech enterprise revenue is **barely trickling in**. When VC funding tightens, the chain collapses from the application layer up, leaving hyperscalers with rapidly depreciating specialized hardware and stranded power contracts.

## Claims it challenges
- That AI software has product-market fit and is generating durable enterprise revenue.
- That the "AI revenue" hyperscalers report on earnings calls reflects sustainable demand.
- The bottom-of-the-U thesis (infrastructure as a safe place to capture margin) — at least under current revenue dynamics.

## Why it matters for *Where Value Lands*
- The single most direct threat to [[H2_u-curve-of-value]]: it argues **the bottom of the U may also be hollow**, propped up by recycled VC. If true, the U-shape is wrong.
- Strongest empirical artillery on the **product layer is structurally unprofitable** claim.
- Connects to scenario 1 ("Hyperscaler consolidation") in [[03_structure]] — Zitron's view implies that scenario ends in a correction, not a stable equilibrium.
