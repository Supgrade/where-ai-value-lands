## Core argument (as inherited)
Using a task-based economic framework and Hulten's theorem, Acemoglu calculates that generative AI will impact <5% of US labor tasks *profitably* over the next decade. TFP gains hit an upper bound of **0.55–0.71% cumulative over 10 years** — orders of magnitude below McKinsey and GS Economists' projections of ~6–9% cumulative GDP/productivity uplift.

He further models:
- AI exerting **downward pressure on real wages** for vulnerable demographics (notably low-education, native-born women).
- A widening **capital-labor income divergence**.
- The proliferation of **negatively-valued tasks** (manipulation, spam, deepfakes) that inflate GDP while reducing welfare.

## Claims it challenges
- Optimistic macroeconomic projections from McKinsey, Goldman Sachs Economists, and similar consultancy/sell-side research.
- The framing of AI as an egalitarian labor-market expansion.
- Any version of "AI doubles GDP" without engaging task-level economics.

## Why it matters for *Where Value Lands*
- Sets a **ceiling on the macroeconomic prize**: if Acemoglu is roughly right, the "redistribution of economic surplus" the paper investigates is operating on a much smaller pie than the optimistic narrative assumes. The question shifts from "how is the surplus divided?" toward "is there a surplus at all, and at whose expense?".
- Provides the rigorous academic spine for the bear case — harder to dismiss than podcaster/Substack critics.
- Feeds directly into [[task-based-framework]] and [[capital-labor-divergence]].
