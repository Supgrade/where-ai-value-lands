# WEF — AI in Action: Beyond Experimentation to Transform Industry (2025)

## Citation

World Economic Forum & Accenture. *AI in Action: Beyond Experimentation to Transform Industry*. Flagship White Paper Series, AI Governance Alliance — Transformation of Industries in the Age of AI. January 2025. 30pp.

Lead authors: Maria Basso, Federico Capaccio, Jill Hoang (WEF); Kathryn White Krumpholz, Karen O'Regan (Accenture).

## Central claim

GenAI investment is surging globally, but the depth of adoption inside organizations is still shallow. The bottleneck is not technology — it is *enterprise readiness*. Scaling AI from pilots into transformation requires a set of foundational enablers (ecosystem collaboration, stakeholder trust, self-governance, talent, cybersecurity, digital core). With these in place, AI moves from operational efficiency toward end-to-end reinvention and eventually value-chain reinvention.

## Stance

Bull case wrapped in responsible-AI rhetoric. Officially: "responsible and scalable transformation." Effectively: the consultancy/multilateral synthesis — Accenture has direct commercial interest in framing the gap as a *managerial* problem (which their advisory practice can fix) rather than an *economic* or *architectural* one. This stance contrasts sharply with [[acemoglu-simple-macroeconomics]], [[zitron-circular-economics]], and [[marcus-world-models-failure]].

The paper does not engage with the bear case at all. It treats the scaling challenge as a sequencing problem, not a structural one.

## Key empirical claims and figures

- **Spending.** Global AI spend ≈ $632B by 2028 (29% CAGR 2024–2028). GenAI spend specifically: ≈ $200B by 2028 (59% CAGR). Source: IDC.
- **Adoption.** 65% of organizations report using genAI in at least one function (McKinsey, 2024). 82% of businesses see genAI as a main lever for reinvention (Accenture).
- **The scaling gap.** **74% of companies report challenges adopting AI at scale; only 16% are prepared for AI-enabled reinvention** (BCG / Accenture, 2024). See [[scaling-gap]].
- **Trust gap.** 95% of workers see value in genAI but worry about positive outcomes; 61% of people hesitate to rely on AI systems (University of Queensland, 2023).
- **Projected economic contribution.** $7.6–17.9 trillion to global economy by 2038 via AI-driven revenue growth (Accenture). *Direct tension* with [[task-based-framework]]'s <0.71% cumulative US TFP ceiling.
- **Productivity outperformance.** Leading AI adopters see 4.5% higher cost-efficiency ratios and 2.4× greater productivity than peers; 15% revenue outperformance projected to more than double by 2026.

## Frameworks contributed to the wiki

### Enterprise adoption phases (5-phase ladder)
A buyer-side organizational maturity ladder distinct from the substrate-ownership [[H1_L0-L7-ladder]]:
- Phase 1: Initial / ad hoc
- Phase 2: "Thousand flowers bloom"
- Phase 3: End-to-end reinvention
- Phase 4: Enterprise-level reinvention
- Phase 5: Value-chain reinvention (future vision)

See [[enterprise-adoption-ladder]] for the full mapping and contrast with L0-L7.

### Foundational enablers
Six enablers required to move beyond Phase 2:
- **Industry-level:** ecosystem collaboration, stakeholder trust
- **Company-level:** self-governance, talent and organization, cybersecurity, digital core

See [[foundational-enablers]].

### Digital core (three layers)
A buyer-perspective stack: (1) AI applications and digital platforms, (2) data and AI backbone, (3) physical and digital infrastructure. Maps roughly onto the upper layers of [[H1_L0-L7-ladder]] — but framed as components the *enterprise buyer* assembles, not as substrate the *vendor* owns. See [[digital-core]] for the dedicated page.

### Adoption archetypes (Taker / Shaper / Maker)
A strategic-positioning typology distinct from the 5-phase maturity ladder above: integrate-off-the-shelf vs. augment-with-proprietary-data vs. train-from-scratch. See [[taker-shaper-maker]].

### Fusion skills
The workforce-capability counterpart to the [[autonomy-slider]]: humans trained to interrogate, judge, and collaborate with AI output. See [[fusion-skills]].

### Industry adoption pattern
Leaders by total AI platform spend: telecommunications, financial services, consumer industries, technology. Forefront of *genAI* spending: human-capital-heavy industries (healthcare, financial services, media/entertainment/sport, consumer, professional services). Rapid catchers-up: mining & metals, automotive & mobility, energy. Lagging: advanced manufacturing.

### Future waves (Section 3)
Three foreseeable waves: (1) full automation of complex repetitive tasks via [[agentic-scaling-law]]-style agents, (2) contextualized/personalized decision-making via reasoning models (cites OpenAI o1), (3) enhanced individual efficiency via edge AI + compact models.

Note: Explicitly cites Anthropic's "computer use" and OpenAI o1. The paper's "agents and multi-agent systems" framing tracks closely with [[karpathy-software-3]] but without the bear-case pressure points.

## Contributions and tensions

**Contributes to the wiki:**
- New empirical lens: [[scaling-gap]] (74% / 16%) — a *measurable* gap between AI investment and AI value-capture inside the enterprise.
- Alternative ladder ([[enterprise-adoption-ladder]]) — buyer-side maturity, complementary to substrate-side [[H1_L0-L7-ladder]].
- Bull-case figures from the consultancy/multilateral establishment, useful as triangulation against [[karpathy-software-3]] (bull-tech) and against the bear cluster.

**Tensions surfaced:**
- WEF projects $7.6–17.9T economic contribution by 2038. [[acemoglu-simple-macroeconomics]] caps US 10-year TFP gains at <0.71% via [[task-based-framework]]. Both cannot be true; both have credibility issues (Accenture has commercial interest; Acemoglu's task-cap model has been challenged for assuming static task definitions).
- WEF assumes scaling is a *managerial* problem solved by enablers. [[zitron-circular-economics]] argues the underlying economics ([[circular-ai-economy]]) don't work regardless of enabler readiness. [[goldman-sachs-too-much-spend]] takes a middle position: too much capex, unclear killer use case, but no judgment on whether enablers fix it.
- WEF makes no mention of the **middle-layer-dies** thesis. Its framing of "new intermediaries and disintermediation" implicitly assumes value flows through the application layer, which is compatible with [[middle-layer-defensibility]] but the paper doesn't engage with the question of where margin actually lands.

**Notable omissions:**
- No geopolitical decomposition. The paper treats AI as a globally homogeneous force, contradicting [[divergent-value-stack-optima]] and [[bradford-digital-empires]].
- No engagement with diffusion vs. innovation ([[diffusion-vs-innovation]]).
- No discussion of capital-labor split ([[capital-labor-divergence]]).
- Treats "responsible AI" as procedural (governance + ethics committees) rather than structural (who captures surplus, who bears costs).

## Quotable passages

> "The goal is not just to automate but to create a relationship where both humans and AI evolve and thrive together." (p. 6)
>
> Reads as boilerplate; useful as the *canonical* consultancy framing of human-AI complementarity. Contrasts with the displacement framing in [[capital-labor-divergence]].

> "74% of companies report challenges in adopting AI at scale, with only 16% of enterprises prepared for AI-enabled reinvention." (p. 13)
>
> The most useful single data point in the paper. The structural question it leaves open: *is the 84% gap fixable with enablers, or is it evidence that enterprise AI value-capture is structurally hard?*

> "AI investments are rising, with genAI adoption still largely in the experimentation stage." (Section 2 header)
>
> The implicit theory of value: investment leads, adoption follows, value materializes. Worth comparing to [[circular-ai-economy]]'s theory: investment is recycled capital and adoption is a marketing artifact.

## Methodology

Mixed: vendor case studies (18 of them, mostly from Accenture/SAP/Databricks/Microsoft customers — selection-biased toward "transformation working"), industry surveys (IDC, BCG, McKinsey, Stanford AI Index, Accenture proprietary), expert interviews via the WEF AI Transformation of Industries community (~400 members from 300+ organizations).

No primary data collection. No counterfactual analysis. No engagement with skeptical literature.
