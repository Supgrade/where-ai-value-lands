# Bradford — Digital Empires

> [!note] Status
> **Stub via synthesis.** This page captures Bradford's contribution as braided into [[geopolitics-global-ai-divide]]. The primary text (*Digital Empires: The Global Battle to Regulate Technology*, Oxford, 2023, plus the Tech Policy Press interview) has not yet been ingested directly.

## Who

Anu Bradford. Columbia Law School. Coined the **Brussels Effect**; later extended it into the *digital empires* framing.

## Central contribution

The world is governed by **three competing digital-empire models**, each exporting a different form of power:
- **US — market-driven**, exports **private corporate power**.
- **China — state-driven**, exports **infrastructure power** (Digital Silk Road).
- **EU — rights-driven**, exports **regulatory power** (Brussels Effect).

See [[digital-empires-tripartite]] for the full unpacking.

## Bradford's diagnosis of Europe

Europe's tech lag is **not caused by regulation**. The deeper structural deficits:
- No truly integrated digital single market.
- No capital markets union; venture capital ecosystem orders of magnitude smaller than the US.
- Cultural attitudes toward risk and failure that suppress founder formation.

This is the strongest available counter to "the AI Act killed European AI" narratives. Regulation is a symptom of a deeper deficit, not its primary cause — though see [[brussels-effect-and-mirage]] for evidence that, in the short term, the AI Act now compounds the deficit.
