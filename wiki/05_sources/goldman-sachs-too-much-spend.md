# Goldman Sachs — Gen AI: Too Much Spend, Too Little Benefit?

> [!info] Citation
> Nathan, Allison and Covello, Jim (lead skeptic voice). *Gen AI: Too Much Spend, Too Little Benefit?* Goldman Sachs *Top of Mind*, Issue 129, June 25, 2024.
> URL: https://www.goldmansachs.com/images/migrated/insights/pages/gs-research/gen-ai--too-much-spend,-too-little-benefit-/TOM_AI%202.0_ForRedaction.pdf

## Core argument (as inherited)
Asks whether ~$1T in AI capital expenditure by hyperscalers and semi vendors can be economically justified. Anchored by Jim Covello (GS Head of Global Equity Research) arguing that gen AI currently produces only **marginal efficiency gains on easily-solvable tasks**, not the complex enterprise-grade problem-solving needed to justify the spend. Compounds the financial argument with **utility, regulatory, and grid constraints** as physical caps on deployment.

## Claims it challenges
- The Silicon Valley narrative that gen AI is a universally applicable killer app comparable to the early internet.
- The implicit assumption that AI inference cost will follow a Moore's Law–style deflationary curve.
- The bull thesis on AI-exposed infrastructure equities.

## Why it matters for *Where Value Lands*
- Comes from the **inside** of capital markets — hard for capital-rich operators (the paper's audience) to dismiss as outsider skepticism.
- Frames the deployment bottleneck as **physical and energetic**, not just economic. This connects directly to the visionary horizon section of [[03_structure]] (energy as the long bottleneck).
- Feeds into [[ai-capex-cycle]] (within [[circular-ai-economy]]) and into any scenario analysis touching infrastructure.
