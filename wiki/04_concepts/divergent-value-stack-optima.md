# Divergent Value-Stack Optima

> [!abstract] Idea
> The AI value stack does not have a single global shape. The U-curve of [[H2_u-curve-of-value]] may be a Western-stack artifact. Each geopolitical bloc engineers a structurally different optimum, dictated by capital structure, regulatory philosophy, and access (or lack of access) to frontier compute.

## The three optima

**Top-heavy (United States).** Value concentrates at hardware (Nvidia) and the foundation-model/cloud layer (Microsoft/OpenAI, Google, AWS, Anthropic). Strategic moat: sheer capital and compute scale. Surplus extracted by metering access to intelligence via closed APIs. Underpinned by national-security framing of AI as an AGI sprint.

**Bottom-heavy (China).** Compute embargo forces a different shape: deliberately commoditize the foundation-model layer via open weights (DeepSeek, Yi-series), push value downstream to application, B2B enterprise integration, and Global-South infrastructure. Strategic moat: diffusion capacity ([[diffusion-vs-innovation]]) + open-weight asymmetry ([[open-weight-asymmetry]]). Goal: be the *"Android of the AI era"*.

**Regulatory-centric (European Union).** No native hyperscalers, no state-directed digital conglomerates. Attempted moat: position the EU as the indispensable arbiter of AI trust via compliance architecture ([[brussels-effect-and-mirage]]) and sovereign hardware ([[sovereign-ai]]). Currently functions more as handicap than moat — capital that should fund compute is funding compliance instead.

## How this modifies the U-curve hypothesis

[[H2_u-curve-of-value]] was authored in the kickoff conversation as a single global shape. Two pressures now stack:

1. **From [[bear-case-synthesis]]:** the bottom of the U may be propped up by VC-recycled cash (see [[circular-ai-economy]]). If so, the U collapses to an L (only the top matters).
2. **From this concept:** even before correction, the U is not universal — it is the **US-specific shape**. China is structurally inverted; the EU has no U at all but a flat plane attempting to bend itself into shape via regulation.

The right framing for the paper is therefore likely:
- The U-curve is one *candidate* shape, native to the US stack under specific capital and regulatory conditions.
- The actual where-does-value-land question must be answered **per bloc**.
- The geographic split (Section 5 of [[03_structure]]) is not a sidebar — it is a structural cross-cut of the whole margin analysis.

## G7-level evidence

The OECD G7 country profiles ([[oecd-sme-ai-adoption-2025]]) reveal **four operationally distinct optima**, not three (see [[g7-sme-ai-policy-pluralism]]):

1. **Market-led** (US): private VC + federal infrastructure (NAIRR, NIST, SBIR/STTR); minimal regulation; 2025 AI Action Plan calls explicitly for *reducing* regulatory burden.
2. **State-coordinated industrial policy** (France, Italy, Germany): EuroHPC AI Factory network, subsidy-driven SME programmes, rights-respecting frameworks; classic EU posture.
3. **State-coordinated + soft-law** (Japan, UK): central direction without hard regulation. Japan's Hiroshima AI Process (HAIP) produced the *International Code of Conduct for Organisations Developing Advanced AI Systems* — a normative leadership move without EU-style horizontal regulation. UK's post-Brexit AI Opportunities Action Plan + Modern Industrial Strategy adopts a similar shape.
4. **Federally-funded, regionally-delivered** (Canada): CIFAR-coordinated, RDA-delivered, with explicit sovereignty framing in compute strategy.

The four optima **converge on operational instruments** (compute access, sectoral subsidies, skills programmes, innovation hubs) even as they diverge in framing. The convergence-with-divergence pattern is itself a finding: governments quote different ideologies but reach for the same toolkit.

## What this would retire

- A finding that the three blocs eventually converge on a single value-capture pattern (e.g., open weights wins everywhere, or closed APIs win everywhere).
- Evidence that China's bottom-heavy strategy is unsustainable without state subsidy and reverts to a US-like top-heavy shape once embargoes lift.
- Evidence that the EU regulatory bet matures into a real economic moat (the long-term version of the "moat or handicap" question).

## Why this might be wrong

- The three "optima" may be the same underlying landscape, photographed from different angles — what looks like top-heavy in the US and bottom-heavy in China may just be the same U-curve with different layers temporarily subsidized.
- The framing may over-credit deliberate engineering. China's bottom-heavy posture is largely a forced response to chip embargoes; the EU's regulatory posture is largely the residual of having neither hyperscalers nor state-directed champions. Both may be *adaptations*, not *optima*.
- The three-bloc model excludes everyone else. India, Gulf states, and Southeast Asia may not fit cleanly into any of these shapes.
