# Foundational Enablers

Six readiness factors that [[wef-ai-in-action-2025]] argues are required to move beyond AI experimentation into transformation. Two operate at the **industry / ecosystem** level; four operate at the **company** level.

## The six enablers

**Industry-level:**
- **Ecosystem collaboration** — strategic partnerships with cloud / AI firms, co-creation, shared data access, risk-and-investment pooling, public-private partnerships. Cited example: EU Digital Europe Programme.
- **Stakeholder trust** — addressing the 61% of people who hesitate to rely on AI; building trust via change management, training, transparent communication.

**Company-level:**
- **Self-governance** — internal ethical standards, chief responsible AI officer, ethics committees, governance embedded in developer tools. Frames itself as *complement* to regulation, not substitute.
- **Talent and organization** — appointing chief AI officers, agile data-centric cultures, "change champions," human-AI interaction roles, continuous learning programs.
- **Cybersecurity** — defending against AI-enabled attacks (deepfakes, phishing, breaches) and using AI to enhance defense; AI security-by-design.
- **[[digital-core]]** — three-layer stack: (1) AI applications and digital platforms, (2) data and AI backbone (including synthetic data, vector DBs, knowledge graphs), (3) physical and digital infrastructure (5G, cloud, edge).

## The implicit theory of value

The framework's load-bearing assumption: **the [[scaling-gap]] is closeable**. If 74% of firms struggle, it is because they lack one or more of the six enablers. Put the enablers in place — usually with consulting help — and the firm moves up the [[enterprise-adoption-ladder]].

The alternative reading is that the framework is itself a **product**: a packaging of consulting services as a maturity checklist. The case studies in the paper are disproportionately drawn from Accenture, SAP, Databricks, and Microsoft customers — the same vendors whose offerings map onto the digital-core layers.

## Related frames

A narrower, SME-specific cousin of the WEF/Accenture six-factor frame is the **OECD four-enabler quartet** (connectivity, AI-enabling inputs, skills, finance) — see [[oecd-sme-enabler-quartet]]. The two frames are complementary:

- The OECD quartet is **demand-side and infrastructure-heavy**: it breaks data/algos/compute apart and adds finance as a first-class enabler, but folds trust and governance into ambient regulation.
- The WEF six-factor is **enterprise-side and governance-heavy**: it surfaces trust, self-governance, cybersecurity, and a thicker "digital core", but assumes balance sheets large enough that finance isn't worth naming.

Mapping: Skills ↔ Talent; Connectivity + AI-enabling inputs ↔ Digital core (broken apart); Finance is OECD-only; Trust + Self-governance + Cybersecurity are WEF-only.

For the white paper: use OECD quartet when discussing diffusion barriers in the SME long tail; use WEF six-factor when discussing why large-enterprise pilots don't translate to ROI.

## What the framework leaves out

- **Distribution.** None of the enablers address whether the enterprise owns its user relationship. [[distribution-moat]] is invisible here.
- **Substrate ownership.** Whether AI runs on the firm's own model or rented capability is treated as a "digital core" implementation detail, not a strategic question. See [[H1_L0-L7-ladder]] for the dimension this collapses.
- **Surplus capture.** The framework asks "how do you implement AI?" not "who captures the surplus it generates?" See [[capital-labor-divergence]] for the missing axis.
- **Geopolitics.** The enablers are presented as universal. The compliance-and-sovereignty story in [[brussels-effect-and-mirage]] and [[sovereign-ai]] suggests they look very different across blocs.
- **Bear-case feasibility.** The framework assumes scaling will work if enablers are in place. [[zitron-circular-economics]], [[task-based-framework]], and [[marcus-world-models-failure]] all argue the constraint is somewhere other than enabler readiness.

## The "Impact / Novelty / Scalability / Responsible AI" criteria

The paper also proposes four criteria for prioritizing AI applications (Box 1, *The Frontier MINDS*):
- **Impact** — business + social outcomes.
- **Novelty** — fresh, combinatorial solutions.
- **Scalability** — adapts across regions / industries.
- **Responsible AI** — aligns with Forum's Digital Trust Framework.

Useful as a buyer-side filter; weak as an analytical lens because the four are not mutually exclusive and none of them resolve the value-capture question.
