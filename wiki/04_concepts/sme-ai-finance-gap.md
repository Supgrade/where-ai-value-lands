# SME AI finance gap

The OECD G7 discussion paper identifies finance as a key enabler of SME AI adoption — and a key bottleneck. SMEs face structural barriers in financing AI investment that are absent for large firms: opacity, asymmetric information, limited credit history, lack of collateral. Layered on top of these structural problems are cyclical ones — recent monetary tightening has cut long-term SME lending in particular, exactly the kind of investment AI adoption requires.

## Why AI is hard to finance for SMEs

AI adoption requires capex (software, possibly hardware), opex (training, hiring, consulting), and the willingness to absorb intangible-asset risk (organisational restructuring, process redesign). For traditional bank lenders this risk profile is unfamiliar and uncollateralisable. The OECD (2015) survey notes that newer, innovative, fast-growing firms with high risk-return profiles are precisely the ones most poorly served by traditional debt — and AI-adopting SMEs sit squarely in that bucket.

## The cyclical squeeze

From the OECD Financing SMEs and Entrepreneurs Scoreboard (2025):

- The cost of SME financing rose at record pace in 2022–2024 due to global uncertainty, inflation, and central-bank tightening.
- Sharp declines in SME lending, **not offset** by asset-based or equity finance.
- A composition shift: more short-term financing for immediate needs, less long-term investment finance — exactly the wrong shape for a multi-year AI integration.

So the macro environment has been actively defunding SME AI investment during the very window when AI is moving from frontier to general-purpose.

## What governments and fintech are doing

The OECD paper documents three responses:

1. **Diversified instruments.** The OECD Recommendation on SME Financing pushes member countries to broaden beyond bank debt — equity, asset-based finance, alternative platforms. Reduces vulnerability to credit-cycle squeezes.
2. **Fintech.** Online platforms partner with traditional lenders; open-banking frameworks enable tailored products. AI itself (credit scoring, automated underwriting) lowers transaction costs and extends credit to thin-file borrowers — including, plausibly, SMEs that traditional credit-scoring would reject.
3. **Non-financial complements.** Investment-readiness programmes, mentoring, advisory services, self-assessment tools. Recognises that for many SMEs the problem isn't capital availability but the firm's capacity to absorb capital productively.

## The fintech double-edge

A subtle point from the OECD/EC (2022) brief: AI-driven credit scoring **can also worsen** financial exclusion if algorithms favour high-return businesses or rely on historical investment patterns. This recapitulates a familiar AI-fairness pattern: same tool, more inclusion or more exclusion depending on whose data it was trained on.

## G7 financing instruments

The country profiles in the OECD paper show heavy reliance on **fiscal-incentive and subsidy** instruments rather than equity:

- **Italy** — Transition 5.0 tax credits, EUR 6bn (NRRP-backed); EUR 350m specifically for SME digital transition through Competence Centres.
- **France** — *IA Booster* subsidising up to 80% of consulting costs; *Osez l'IA* EUR 200m for adoption.
- **Germany** — *KI4KMU* up to 50% of project costs; "Generative AI for SMEs" ~EUR 30m.
- **UK** — Flexible AI Upskilling Fund (up to 50%); GBP 3bn capital boost to the British Business Bank; 69,000 start-up loans.
- **Japan** — Catalog-Type Labor-Saving Investment Subsidy ¥500bn; General-Type Subsidies cover up to 50% of cost for flexible AI/automation.
- **US** — SBIR/STTR ("America's Seed Fund") for non-dilutive grants; no broad subsidy programme. Most US AI capital flows through private VC, not federal SME programmes.

Note the cross-country pattern: **EU bloc relies on grants and tax credits; US relies on private capital with seed-fund backstop; Japan deploys catalogue-style turnkey subsidies**. This maps onto [[digital-empires-tripartite]] surprisingly cleanly — but with Japan as a fourth, distinct model.
