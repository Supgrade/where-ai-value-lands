# OECD Sectoral AI Adoption 2024

## Headline figures

OECD-wide AI adoption rates by sector, 2024 (firms with 10+ employees, unweighted average):

| Sector | 2021 | 2024 | Δ (pp) |
|--------|------|------|--------|
| **Information and communication (ICT)** | ~24.5% | **~44.5%** | +20.0 |
| **Professional, scientific & technical** | ~13.0% | **~26.0%** | +13.0 |
| Wholesale trade | ~6.5% | ~14.8% | +8.3 |
| Administrative & support services | ~7.5% | ~15.0% | +7.5 |
| Real estate | ~5.7% | ~13.3% | +7.6 |
| Retail trade | ~5.2% | ~11.5% | +6.3 |
| Manufacturing | ~7.0% | ~12.3% | +5.3 |
| Transportation & storage | ~5.6% | ~9.2% | +3.6 |
| Accommodation & food services | ~3.1% | ~7.8% | +4.7 |
| **Construction** | ~3.2% | **~7.2%** | +4.0 |

Source figure: [[oecd-sme-ai-adoption-2025]], Figure 4, p. 13.

**Pattern:** ICT and Professional services adopt AI at 3–6× the rate of Construction, Hospitality, and Transportation. Every sector roughly doubled adoption 2021→2024; the **absolute gap between leading and lagging sectors widened** (ICT pulled away by ~15 pp).

## Sectoral AI-intensity decomposition (Calvino et al. 2024)

Four-dimensional AI-intensity indicator across 38 A38 sectors (manufacturing ex. coke/petroleum, construction, business services). Top-quartile (dark navy) across **all four** indicators (human capital, innovation, exposure, use):

- **Media (58–60)**
- **Telecommunications (61)**
- **IT services (62–63)**
- **Computer & electronics (26)**
- **Scientific R&D (72)**

Top-quartile on three or more:
- Finance & insurance (64–66)
- Legal & accounting (69–71)
- Other business services (73–75)

Bottom-quartile (white / lightest) across most indicators:
- Food products (10–12)
- Textiles & apparel (13–15)
- Wood & paper (16–18)
- Hotels & food services (55–56)
- Construction (41–43) — sometimes second quartile on use

Mid-tier (mixed):
- Pharmaceuticals (21) — top on human capital; lower on exposure
- Electrical / machinery / transport-equipment manufacturing — second to third quartile

Source: Calvino et al. (2024), reproduced in [[oecd-sme-ai-adoption-2025]], Figure 5, p. 14.

## G7 stratification

OECD reports that in G7 economies, **ICT sector adoption is typically more than 3× manufacturing sector adoption**. This holds for all G7 countries; per-country detail in the source's Table A.2 / Annex.

## Caveats

- "Sector" maps onto NACE / ISIC categories; comparability across statistical agencies is approximate.
- AI definitions broadened over 2021–2024 (generative AI now explicitly captured).
- The Calvino et al. AI-intensity index is a *composite* — different weighting choices produce different rank orders, particularly in the middle of the distribution.

## Why this matters

- **Sectoral concentration compounds the [[sme-ai-adoption-gap]].** SMEs are over-represented in low-AI-intensity sectors (construction, hospitality, transport, retail) — so part of the size gap is a sector compositional effect. OECD work shows the size gap *persists within sector*, but the sectoral pattern multiplies it.
- **The AI-producing sectors are the AI-adopting sectors.** ICT, Media, IT services lead on both innovation and use — the part of the economy that makes AI is the part that captures most surplus today. Consistent with [[capital-labor-divergence]].
- **The diffusion question is at the lagging sectors.** Per [[diffusion-vs-innovation]], the GPT winner is whoever absorbs the technology across all sectors. The pattern shows OECD economies are still early on the absorption curve for construction, manufacturing, transport, hospitality.
