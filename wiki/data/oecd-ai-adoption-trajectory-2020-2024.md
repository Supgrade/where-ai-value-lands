# OECD AI Adoption Trajectory 2020–2024

## Headline figures

OECD-wide share of firms with 10+ employees using AI:

| Year | Share |
|------|-------|
| 2020 | 5.6% |
| 2021 | 7.3% |
| 2022 | 8.1% |
| 2023 | 8.5% |
| 2024 | **14.0%** |

**OECD-wide AI adoption roughly 2.5× over five years; the largest year-on-year jump (8.5% → 14%) is 2023→2024, coinciding with the generative-AI inflection.**

Source figure: [[oecd-sme-ai-adoption-2025]], Figure 1, p. 9 (OECD ICT Access and Usage Database, accessed July 2025).

## Comparison to other digital technologies

| Technology | 2024 OECD adoption |
|------------|--------------------|
| Cloud computing | >50% |
| **AI** | **14%** |

AI is well below the diffusion level of mature digital technologies. It is still on the **steep early part of the S-curve**; cloud computing is past its steepest part.

## Caveats

- Unweighted average across OECD Member countries; country composition varies year to year as data become available.
- Definitions of "AI use" have expanded over the period — 2024 survey instruments capture generative AI explicitly, which earlier instruments do not. Some of the 2023→2024 jump is real adoption acceleration; some is definition broadening.
- Cross-country comparability caveats apply throughout.

## Why this matters

- **The trajectory is real but the level is low.** AI is not (yet) a generalized firm-level technology. At 14% with the gap widening by firm size, the OECD economy is still on the early phase of the diffusion curve.
- **The gap is widening in absolute terms.** Even as adoption grows, the gap between small-firm and large-firm adoption rates is increasing in percentage-point terms — a structural divergence, not a temporary lag.
- **Per [[diffusion-vs-innovation]], the trajectory is the load-bearing metric.** If the curve continues to flatten at the SME tail, the diffusion-deficit hypothesis hardens.
