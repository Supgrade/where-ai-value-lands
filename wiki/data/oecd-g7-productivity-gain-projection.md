# OECD G7 AI Productivity Gain Projection (Filippucci et al. 2025)

## Headline figure

**Annual labour-productivity growth from AI across G7 economies could range from 0.2 to 1.3 percentage points over the next decade**, depending on the adoption scenario.

For reference: the US ICT boom of the mid-1990s delivered an estimated **0.5–1.5 pp** of annual labour-productivity growth.

Source: Filippucci et al. (2025), OECD working paper, cited in [[oecd-sme-ai-adoption-2025]] p. 14.

## Cumulative over a decade

The mid-point of the projection range, ~0.7 pp annual labour-productivity growth, **cumulates to roughly 7% over a decade** of compounded growth — far above the upper end of Acemoglu's task-based macro estimate ([[task-based-framework]], <0.71% cumulative TFP).

The lower end of the projection (0.2 pp, ~2% cumulative) is closer to but still above Acemoglu's range.

## Caveats

- **Projection, not measurement.** Filippucci et al. is a forward-looking model with explicit adoption-scenario dependencies. The 1.3 pp top of the range assumes aggressive diffusion across sectors, including SMEs.
- **Currently-observed adoption levels do not support the high scenario.** With OECD-wide AI adoption at 14% in 2024 and core-business adoption at 1.9–6.1% across G7 in 2024, the diffusion pace required for the high scenario is implausibly fast.
- **Headline firm-level premia do not aggregate cleanly.** Within-cohort labour-productivity premia of >4–15% for AI adopters (vs comparable non-adopters) does *not* mechanically translate to 0.2–1.3 pp macro gains — most firms have not adopted, and the J-curve drag is unmodelled. See [[ai-productivity-firm-level]].
- **Selection effects.** Firms that adopt AI are already more productive; some of the AI premium is selection, not treatment. The macro projection inherits this issue.

## Why this matters

- **It is the bull-multilateral number.** The OECD figure is the canonical official-international-body productivity projection for AI. It will be cited as the consensus by policymakers.
- **It is incompatible with the bear cap.** [[task-based-framework]] caps US cumulative TFP gains from AI at <0.71% over a decade. The OECD projection's mid- and upper-end are 5–10× this. **The two views remain unreconciled** and the gap between them is one of the most important open questions in the wiki.
- **It depends on the SME diffusion question.** The high scenario requires diffusion deep into the SME tail — which [[sme-ai-adoption-gap]] shows has barely started. The macro projection is *conditional on* solving the diffusion problem the same paper documents.
