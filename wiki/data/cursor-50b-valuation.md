# Cursor — $50B valuation, $2B ARR

## The data

> **Anysphere (Cursor) — $50B valuation, $2B ARR** as of early 2026.

One of the fastest-growing enterprise software companies in history. The "automobile vs. engine" framing (Michael Truell): while foundation-model labs build engines, Cursor builds the complete car developers actually drive.

The moat components:
- Intelligent model routing across third-party and proprietary models.
- Continuous learning pipeline from real developer interactions.
- Context-aware codebase adaptation (the AI understands the full organizational codebase, not isolated snippets).
- Enterprise-embedded workflows (Bugbot, security agent fleets).

## Source

Synthesized in [[where-value-lands-2026]] from Michael Truell's appearances on Lenny's Podcast and Cursor's blog. The $50B / $2B ARR figures are widely reported in trade press through 2025–early 2026.

## What it shows

The single sharpest empirical disconfirmation of the naïve "middle dies" thesis, **conditional** on what "middle" means. Cursor occupies the IDE / orchestration layer but behaves like the top of the stack by function: distribution ownership, workflow lock-in, proprietary data flywheel.

The paper's [[H2_u-curve-of-value]] hypothesis is partially validated *and* partially complicated by this data point: the top of the U holds, but its definition has to shift from "literal application surface" to "any company with distribution + proprietary data flywheel."
