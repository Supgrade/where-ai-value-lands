# Acemoglu's <0.71% cumulative TFP ceiling

## The data

> **Upper-bound 10-year US Total Factor Productivity gain from generative AI: 0.55–0.71% cumulative.**
> **Upper-bound 10-year US GDP gain: 0.9–1.6% cumulative.**

Derived via Hulten's theorem from a task-decomposition of the US economy. **For comparison**, optimistic forecasts (McKinsey, Goldman Sachs Economists) put 10-year US productivity gains near 9% cumulative — **>10× higher**.

Adjusted for the easy-to-learn / hard-to-learn task distinction (hard tasks resist current AI architectures), the realistic ceiling pulls down toward **<0.55% cumulative**.

## Source

Daron Acemoglu, *The Simple Macroeconomics of AI*, NBER Working Paper 32487, May 2024. Surfaced through: [[acemoglu-simple-macroeconomics]] and synthesized in [[bear-case-synthesis]].

## What it shows

A formal lower-bound on the macro impact of generative AI under standard labor-economics assumptions. Even if every task-level AI demonstration is real, the aggregate productivity gain is bounded by *(fraction of tasks impacted) × (average task-level cost saving)* — which the paper estimates as ≈ 4.6% × 15.4%.

It is **the single bear-case number most in direct tension with [[wef-7-6t-projection]]**. Both cannot simultaneously be right.
